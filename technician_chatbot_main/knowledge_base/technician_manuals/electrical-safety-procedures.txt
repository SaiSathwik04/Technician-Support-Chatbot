# Electrical Safety Procedures - Industrial Maintenance

## SECTION 1: ELECTRICAL SAFETY OVERVIEW
This manual provides comprehensive electrical safety procedures for maintenance personnel working on industrial electrical systems. All procedures must comply with NFPA 70E and OSHA 1910 Subpart S requirements.

## SECTION 2: LOCKOUT/TAGOUT (LOTO) PROCEDURES

### 2.1 PREPARATION FOR LOTO
**Before Beginning Work:**
1. Identify all energy sources that could cause injury
2. Notify all affected personnel of planned work
3. Gather required LOTO devices and tools
4. Review equipment-specific energy control procedure
5. Ensure adequate lighting and access to work area

### 2.2 STEP-BY-STEP LOTO PROCEDURE

**Step 1: Equipment Shutdown**
1. Follow normal equipment shutdown sequence
2. Allow all moving parts to come to complete stop
3. Ensure all stored energy is released safely
4. Verify equipment is in zero-energy state

**Step 2: Energy Source Isolation**
1. Operate all energy-isolating devices:
   - Main electrical disconnects
   - Circuit breakers and fuses
   - Pneumatic supply valves
   - Hydraulic shutoff valves
   - Steam isolation valves
   - Gas supply valves

2. Apply LOTO devices:
   - Individual padlocks on each energy source
   - Danger tags with worker information
   - Multiple lockout devices if needed
   - Group lockout for team work

**Step 3: Verification of Isolation**
1. Attempt to start equipment (should not operate)
2. Use digital multimeter to verify zero voltage
3. Test voltage tester on known live source first
4. Check for stored energy in:
   - Capacitors and electrical circuits
   - Compressed air systems
   - Hydraulic accumulators
   - Elevated equipment or counterweights

### 2.3 RESTORATION PROCEDURES
**After Work Completion:**
1. Remove all tools and materials from work area
2. Reinstall all guards and safety devices
3. Ensure all personnel are clear of equipment
4. Remove LOTO devices (only by person who applied them)
5. Restore energy sources in proper sequence
6. Test equipment operation before returning to service

## SECTION 3: PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT (PPE)

### 3.1 ARC FLASH PROTECTION
**Arc Flash Risk Assessment Required for:**
- Work on or near energized electrical conductors
- Equipment operating at 50 volts or higher
- Tasks that could result in electric arc

**PPE Categories (NFPA 70E):**
- **Category 1:** 4 cal/cm² minimum
  - Arc-rated long-sleeve shirt and pants
  - Arc-rated face shield or hood
  - Safety glasses, hard hat, leather gloves

- **Category 2:** 8 cal/cm² minimum
  - Arc-rated shirt and pants
  - Arc-rated face shield and balaclava
  - Safety glasses, hard hat, leather gloves

- **Category 3:** 25 cal/cm² minimum
  - Arc-rated shirt and pants
  - Arc-rated coverall or arc flash suit
  - Arc-rated face shield and balaclava
  - Safety glasses, hard hat, leather gloves

- **Category 4:** 40 cal/cm² minimum
  - Arc-rated shirt and pants
  - Arc-rated coverall and arc flash suit
  - Arc-rated face shield and balaclava
  - Safety glasses, hard hat, leather gloves

### 3.2 ELECTRICAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT

**Insulated Tools:**
- Voltage rating appropriate for system
- Inspect before each use for damage
- Test dielectric strength annually
- Store in protective cases when not in use

**Voltage-Rated Gloves:**
- Class 00: Up to 500V AC
- Class 0: Up to 1,000V AC
- Class 1: Up to 7,500V AC
- Class 2: Up to 17,000V AC
- Class 3: Up to 26,500V AC
- Class 4: Up to 36,000V AC

**Glove Care and Testing:**
- Air test before each use
- Visual inspection for cuts, tears, ozone damage
- Electrical testing every 6 months
- Use leather protectors over rubber gloves

## SECTION 4: ELECTRICAL TESTING PROCEDURES

### 4.1 VOLTAGE TESTING
**Before Testing:**
1. Verify test instrument on known live source
2. Select appropriate voltage range
3. Use proper test leads and probes
4. Wear appropriate PPE for voltage level

**Testing Procedure:**
1. Approach conductor carefully
2. Test each phase to ground
3. Test phase to phase voltages
4. Record all measurements
5. Verify test instrument still functional after testing

### 4.2 INSULATION RESISTANCE TESTING
**Equipment Required:**
- Megohmmeter rated for system voltage
- Test leads with appropriate voltage rating
- Recording forms for documentation

**Testing Procedure:**
1. De-energize and lock out circuit
2. Disconnect equipment from power source
3. Discharge any capacitors
4. Connect megger test leads
5. Apply test voltage for 1 minute
6. Record insulation resistance values

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- New installations: >100 megohms
- Existing equipment: >1 megohm per 1000V + 1 megohm
- Minimum acceptable: 1 megohm

### 4.3 CONTINUITY TESTING
**Purpose:** Verify conductor integrity and grounding paths

**Procedure:**
1. De-energize circuit completely
2. Set multimeter to resistance (ohms) function
3. Test conductor end-to-end
4. Verify ground conductor continuity
5. Check bonding jumper connections

## SECTION 5: WORKING ON ENERGIZED EQUIPMENT

### 5.1 JUSTIFICATION FOR ENERGIZED WORK
**Energized work permitted only when:**
- De-energizing creates greater hazards
- Required for troubleshooting and testing
- Work on continuous process systems
- Written justification approved by management

### 5.2 ENERGIZED WORK PERMITS
**Required Elements:**
- Description of work to be performed
- Justification for energized work
- Risk assessment and safety measures
- PPE requirements based on incident energy
- Qualified person approval signatures
- Emergency response procedures

### 5.3 APPROACH BOUNDARIES
**Limited Approach Boundary:**
- Distance where unqualified person may not approach
- Based on system voltage level
- Training required to cross this boundary

**Restricted Approach Boundary:**
- Distance where qualified person may not approach
- Additional PPE required
- Supervisor approval needed

**Prohibited Approach Boundary:**
- Considered same as contact with energized conductor
- Only qualified persons with special training
- Maximum PPE requirements

## SECTION 6: ELECTRICAL SYSTEM MAINTENANCE

### 6.1 MOTOR CONTROL CENTER (MCC) MAINTENANCE
**Quarterly Inspection:**
- Visual inspection of bus bars for corrosion
- Check control wire connections
- Test operation of contactors and overloads
- Verify proper ventilation and cooling
- Clean dust and debris from enclosures

**Annual Maintenance:**
- Torque all bolted connections per manufacturer specs
- Test insulation resistance of bus work
- Calibrate protective relays
- Test ground fault protection systems
- Update electrical drawings as needed

### 6.2 TRANSFORMER MAINTENANCE
**Monthly Checks:**
- Record loading levels
- Check cooling system operation
- Monitor oil level and temperature
- Inspect for leaks and unusual noises
- Verify tap changer operation

**Annual Testing:**
- Oil analysis for dielectric strength
- Insulation resistance testing
- Turns ratio testing
- Impedance testing
- Ground resistance verification

### 6.3 SWITCHGEAR MAINTENANCE
**Semi-Annual Inspection:**
- Check all mechanical operations
- Lubricate operating mechanisms
- Test protective relay calibration
- Verify control power supply
- Inspect for tracking and corona damage

**Biennial Testing:**
- Primary injection testing of protective relays
- Dielectric testing of circuit breakers
- Contact resistance measurements
- Timing tests of breaker operations
- Insulation resistance testing

## SECTION 7: EMERGENCY PROCEDURES

### 7.1 ELECTRICAL SHOCK RESPONSE
**If Person is Still in Contact with Electricity:**
1. Do NOT touch the victim
2. Turn off power source immediately
3. If power cannot be turned off, use insulated implement to separate victim
4. Call for emergency medical assistance
5. Begin CPR if qualified and victim needs it

**After Victim is Separated from Electrical Source:**
1. Check for consciousness and breathing
2. Look for entrance and exit wounds
3. Monitor for signs of shock
4. Keep victim warm and lying down
5. Do not give food or water

### 7.2 ELECTRICAL FIRE RESPONSE
**Class C Electrical Fires:**
1. De-energize equipment if safely possible
2. Use only Class C fire extinguishers on energized equipment
3. Never use water on energized electrical equipment
4. Evacuate area if fire cannot be controlled quickly
5. Call fire department even for small fires

**After De-energizing:**
- Class A extinguishers may be used
- Water can be used if equipment is completely de-energized
- Be aware of potential re-energization

### 7.3 ARC FLASH INCIDENT
**Immediate Response:**
1. Shut off power source if safely accessible
2. Call emergency medical services
3. Do not touch victim until power is confirmed off
4. Remove victim from area if safe to do so
5. Treat for burns and shock

**Follow-up Actions:**
- Investigate cause of arc flash
- Review and update arc flash study
- Retrain personnel as needed
- Inspect equipment for damage

## SECTION 8: ELECTRICAL SAFETY TRAINING

### 8.1 QUALIFIED PERSON REQUIREMENTS
**Training Must Include:**
- Electrical hazard recognition
- Proper use of PPE and test equipment
- LOTO procedures and verification
- Arc flash hazard assessment
- Emergency response procedures
- Hands-on practical exercises

**Retraining Required:**
- Every 3 years minimum
- When new hazards are introduced
- After any electrical incident
- When job assignments change

### 8.2 UNQUALIFIED PERSON TRAINING
**Basic Electrical Safety:**
- Recognition of electrical hazards
- Understanding of approach boundaries
- When to notify qualified persons
- Basic emergency response
- Use of appropriate PPE

## SECTION 9: ELECTRICAL CODES AND STANDARDS

### 9.1 APPLICABLE STANDARDS
- **NFPA 70E:** Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace
- **NFPA 70:** National Electrical Code (NEC)
- **OSHA 1910.331-335:** Electrical Safety Standards
- **IEEE 1584:** Guide for Arc Flash Hazard Calculations
- **ASTM F496:** Standard Specification for In-Service Care of Insulating Gloves

### 9.2 RECORD KEEPING REQUIREMENTS
**Required Documentation:**
- Employee training records
- Equipment maintenance records
- Electrical testing results
- Incident investigation reports
- PPE inspection and testing records
- LOTO procedure updates

**Retention Periods:**
- Training records: 3 years minimum
- Testing records: Life of equipment
- Incident reports: 5 years minimum
- PPE testing: Until next test due

This electrical safety manual provides essential procedures for safe work on industrial electrical systems. All personnel must be properly trained and follow these procedures without exception.
