# Pump Troubleshooting Manual - Industrial Systems

## SECTION 1: PUMP OVERVIEW
This manual covers troubleshooting procedures for centrifugal pumps, positive displacement pumps, and specialty pumps commonly used in industrial applications.

## SECTION 2: CENTRIFUGAL PUMP TROUBLESHOOTING

### 2.1 PUMP WON'T PRIME OR START
**Symptoms:** Pump runs but no flow, cavitation noise, excessive vibration

**Possible Causes:**
- Air leaks in suction line
- Pump not filled with liquid
- Suction lift too high
- Foot valve stuck closed
- Impeller clogged
- Wrong rotation direction

**Diagnostic Steps:**
1. **Check Suction Line Integrity:**
   - Inspect all joints for air leaks
   - Test suction line under vacuum
   - Check packing gland for air entry
   - Verify foot valve operation

2. **Verify Pump Priming:**
   - Fill pump casing completely with liquid
   - Vent all air from casing and suction line
   - Check priming valve operation
   - Ensure suction line slopes upward to pump

3. **Calculate Net Positive Suction Head (NPSH):**
   - NPSH Available = Atmospheric Pressure + Static Head - Vapor Pressure - Friction Losses
   - Must exceed NPSH Required by 2-3 feet minimum
   - Reduce suction lift if necessary

### 2.2 LOW FLOW OR PRESSURE
**Symptoms:** Reduced output, poor performance, increased power consumption

**Possible Causes:**
- Impeller wear or damage
- Internal wear ring clearances excessive
- Discharge valve partially closed
- Suction strainer clogged
- Speed too low
- Specific gravity higher than design

**Diagnostic Steps:**
1. **Impeller Inspection:**
   - Remove pump casing to inspect impeller
   - Check for erosion, corrosion, or mechanical damage
   - Measure impeller diameter (normal wear: 5% reduction maximum)
   - Check impeller-to-casing clearances

2. **Performance Testing:**
   - Install pressure gauges at suction and discharge
   - Measure flow rate with calibrated flow meter
   - Calculate pump head: H = (P₂ - P₁)/γ + (V₂² - V₁²)/2g + (Z₂ - Z₁)
   - Compare to manufacturer's curve

3. **System Analysis:**
   - Check all valves in discharge line
   - Inspect and clean suction strainer
   - Verify pump speed with tachometer
   - Check coupling for slippage

### 2.3 EXCESSIVE POWER CONSUMPTION
**Symptoms:** High motor current, tripped overloads, increased energy costs

**Possible Causes:**
- Pump running beyond best efficiency point
- Impeller diameter too large
- Liquid density higher than expected
- Misalignment
- Worn bearings
- Damaged impeller causing turbulence

**Diagnostic Steps:**
1. **Operating Point Analysis:**
   - Plot current operating point on pump curve
   - Check if operating at low flow/high head (left of BEP)
   - Verify system head curve matches pump selection

2. **Mechanical Inspection:**
   - Check shaft alignment with dial indicators
   - Test bearing condition with vibration analysis
   - Inspect coupling for wear or damage
   - Measure motor current and compare to nameplate

### 2.4 CAVITATION PROBLEMS
**Symptoms:** Noise like gravel in pump, vibration, reduced performance, pitting damage

**Possible Causes:**
- Insufficient NPSH available
- Suction pressure too low
- Liquid temperature too high
- Pump operating at excessive flow
- Suction line restrictions

**Diagnostic Steps:**
1. **NPSH Calculation:**
   - Measure suction pressure at pump inlet
   - Check liquid temperature and vapor pressure
   - Calculate friction losses in suction piping
   - Compare NPSH available to NPSH required

2. **System Modifications:**
   - Lower pump elevation
   - Increase suction pipe diameter
   - Reduce suction line length
   - Install suction stabilizer if necessary
   - Throttle discharge valve to reduce flow

## SECTION 3: POSITIVE DISPLACEMENT PUMP TROUBLESHOOTING

### 3.1 RECIPROCATING PUMPS
**Common Issues:**
- Valve failures (suction/discharge)
- Packing leakage
- Pulsation problems
- Rod/crosshead wear

**Diagnostic Procedures:**
1. **Valve Testing:**
   - Remove valve covers and inspect seats
   - Check valve spring tension
   - Test valve lift and timing
   - Replace worn or damaged valves

2. **Packing Maintenance:**
   - Adjust packing gland gradually
   - Replace packing rings in sets
   - Check shaft sleeve condition
   - Maintain slight leakage for lubrication

### 3.2 ROTARY PUMPS (Gear, Screw, Vane)
**Common Issues:**
- Excessive internal leakage
- Wear of rotating elements
- Bearing failures
- Seal problems

**Diagnostic Procedures:**
1. **Clearance Checks:**
   - Measure clearances with feeler gauges
   - Check gear/rotor-to-housing clearances
   - Verify end plate clearances
   - Replace worn components as needed

2. **Performance Testing:**
   - Measure slip rate at various pressures
   - Check volumetric efficiency
   - Monitor bearing temperatures
   - Verify seal effectiveness

## SECTION 4: BEARING AND SEAL MAINTENANCE

### 4.1 BEARING PROBLEMS
**Symptoms:** High temperature, noise, vibration, grease leakage

**Causes and Solutions:**
- **Overlubrication:** Remove excess grease, establish proper relubrication schedule
- **Underlubrication:** Add specified grease quantity, check automatic lubricators
- **Contamination:** Replace bearings and seals, improve environmental protection
- **Misalignment:** Realign pump and driver, check foundation settlement

**Bearing Replacement Procedure:**
1. Remove old bearings with proper pullers
2. Clean bearing housing thoroughly
3. Heat new bearings to 80-100°C
4. Install with proper tools (not hammers)
5. Apply correct grease quantity
6. Verify shaft runout <0.002 inches

### 4.2 MECHANICAL SEAL MAINTENANCE
**Common Seal Failures:**
- **Face wear:** Hard particles in liquid, insufficient lubrication
- **Secondary seal leakage:** Chemical attack, installation damage
- **Spring failure:** Corrosion, fatigue, improper installation

**Seal Installation Best Practices:**
1. Clean all surfaces thoroughly
2. Handle seal faces carefully - no contact
3. Use proper installation tools
4. Check seal chamber dimensions
5. Verify flushing arrangements
6. Follow manufacturer's torque specifications

## SECTION 5: PUMP PERFORMANCE ANALYSIS

### 5.1 EFFICIENCY CALCULATIONS
**Overall Efficiency = (Water HP / Brake HP) × 100**

Where:
- Water HP = (Flow × Head × Specific Gravity) / 3960
- Brake HP = Motor HP × Motor Efficiency × Motor Loading

**Typical Efficiencies:**
- Small pumps (<10 HP): 50-70%
- Medium pumps (10-100 HP): 70-85%
- Large pumps (>100 HP): 80-90%

### 5.2 SYSTEM CURVE ANALYSIS
**System Head = Static Head + Friction Head**
- Static Head: Elevation difference + pressure difference
- Friction Head: Pipe friction + fitting losses + valve losses

**Friction Loss Calculation:**
Hazen-Williams Formula: h = 10.67 × L × Q^1.852 / (C^1.852 × d^4.87)

Where:
- h = head loss (feet)
- L = pipe length (feet)
- Q = flow rate (GPM)
- C = pipe roughness coefficient
- d = pipe diameter (inches)

## SECTION 6: PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE SCHEDULE

### 6.1 DAILY CHECKS
- Monitor pump discharge pressure
- Check bearing housing temperature
- Listen for unusual noises
- Verify proper motor current
- Inspect for leaks at seals and flanges
- Check cooling water flow (if applicable)

### 6.2 WEEKLY MAINTENANCE
- Lubricate bearings per schedule
- Check and record vibration levels
- Inspect coupling condition
- Verify alignment marks
- Clean strainers and filters
- Check automatic control operation

### 6.3 MONTHLY MAINTENANCE
- Measure and record performance data
- Check motor current and compare to baseline
- Inspect foundation for cracks or settlement
- Test emergency shutdown systems
- Review maintenance records and trends

### 6.4 QUARTERLY MAINTENANCE
- Precision alignment check
- Comprehensive vibration analysis
- Thermographic inspection
- Oil analysis (oil-lubricated bearings)
- Overhaul relief valves and controls

### 6.5 ANNUAL MAINTENANCE
- Complete pump disassembly and inspection
- Replace wear parts as needed
- Pressure test all pressure vessels
- Calibrate instrumentation
- Update maintenance procedures

## SECTION 7: SAFETY PROCEDURES

### 7.1 LOCKOUT/TAGOUT REQUIREMENTS
**Before Any Maintenance:**
1. Shut down pump and lock out power
2. Close and lock suction and discharge valves
3. Relieve all pressure in system
4. Drain pump casing if required
5. Test for hazardous atmospheres
6. Apply personal danger tags

### 7.2 CONFINED SPACE ENTRY
**For Pump Pit Work:**
- Test atmosphere for oxygen, toxic gases, explosives
- Use continuous air monitoring
- Maintain attendant outside space
- Use proper ventilation and lighting
- Have rescue equipment readily available

### 7.3 CHEMICAL HANDLING
**When Working with Process Fluids:**
- Know material safety data sheet requirements
- Use appropriate PPE for chemical exposure
- Have eyewash and safety shower available
- Use proper decontamination procedures
- Follow waste disposal regulations

## SECTION 8: EMERGENCY PROCEDURES

### 8.1 PUMP SEAL FAILURE
1. Shut down pump immediately
2. Close discharge valve
3. Isolate pump from system
4. Contain any spilled liquid
5. Assess environmental impact
6. Plan repair strategy

### 8.2 BEARING FAILURE
1. Stop pump immediately if safe to do so
2. Allow bearing to cool before inspection
3. Check for shaft damage
4. Assess cause of failure
5. Plan bearing replacement
6. Check alignment before restart

### 8.3 CAVITATION DAMAGE
1. Reduce pump flow immediately
2. Check suction pressure
3. Lower pump speed if possible
4. Inspect impeller for damage
5. Calculate required NPSH margin
6. Plan system modifications if needed

This troubleshooting manual provides systematic approaches to diagnosing and resolving common pump problems in industrial applications.
