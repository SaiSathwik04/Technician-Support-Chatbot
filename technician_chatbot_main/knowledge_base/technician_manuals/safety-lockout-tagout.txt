# Safety Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) Procedures - Industrial Maintenance

## SECTION 1: LOTO PROGRAM OVERVIEW
This manual establishes comprehensive lockout/tagout procedures to prevent unexpected startup of machinery during maintenance, ensuring worker safety in compliance with OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147.

## SECTION 2: SCOPE AND DEFINITIONS

### 2.1 SCOPE OF APPLICATION
**This program applies to:**
- All mechanical, electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic, chemical, and thermal energy sources
- Equipment maintenance, repair, cleaning, and servicing activities
- Installation of new equipment or modification of existing systems
- Any activity where unexpected energy release could cause injury

**Energy Sources Covered:**
- **Electrical:** Main power, control circuits, UPS systems, stored electrical energy
- **Mechanical:** Rotating equipment, springs, elevated components, pressurized systems
- **Hydraulic:** Pressurized fluid systems, accumulators, elevated cylinders
- **Pneumatic:** Compressed air systems, vacuum systems, stored air pressure
- **Thermal:** Steam, hot water, heated surfaces, cryogenic systems
- **Chemical:** Process chemicals, stored chemical energy, chemical reactions
- **Gravitational:** Elevated equipment, counterweights, suspended loads

### 2.2 KEY DEFINITIONS
- **Authorized Employee:** Worker trained and designated to perform LOTO procedures
- **Affected Employee:** Worker whose job requires operation of machinery subject to LOTO
- **Energy Isolating Device:** Physical device that prevents energy transmission
- **Lockout Device:** Device that uses positive means (lock) to hold energy isolating device
- **Tagout Device:** Prominent warning device (tag) attached to energy isolating device

## SECTION 3: ENERGY CONTROL PROCEDURES

### 3.1 PREPARATION FOR LOCKOUT
**Before Beginning Work:**
1. **Identify Equipment:** Locate and identify all energy sources
2. **Review Procedures:** Study equipment-specific energy control procedure
3. **Gather Materials:** Collect required locks, tags, and tools
4. **Notify Personnel:** Inform all affected employees of planned work
5. **Plan Emergency Response:** Establish communication and emergency procedures

**Required Information:**
- Equipment identification and location
- Type and magnitude of energy sources
- Methods for energy isolation and verification
- Specific hazards associated with re-energization
- Required personal protective equipment

### 3.2 SEQUENCE OF LOCKOUT PROCEDURES

**Step 1: Equipment Preparation**
1. Notify all affected employees of impending lockout
2. Shut down equipment using normal operating procedures
3. Allow all moving parts to come to complete stop
4. Verify equipment is in zero-energy state
5. Post warning signs at control points

**Step 2: Equipment Isolation**
1. Locate and operate all energy isolating devices
2. Move isolating devices to "SAFE" or "OFF" position
3. Ensure isolation devices are in safe position
4. Verify no inadvertent operation is possible

**Common Energy Isolating Devices:**
- Circuit breakers and disconnect switches
- Line valves and block valves
- Pneumatic supply valves
- Hydraulic shutoff valves
- Steam isolation valves
- Gas supply valves

**Step 3: Lockout/Tagout Application**
1. Apply individual lockout devices to each energy source
2. Attach danger tags with appropriate information
3. Ensure locks cannot be bypassed or removed
4. Use hasps for multiple person lockout
5. Apply locks in manner that prevents operation

**Required Tag Information:**
- Name of authorized employee
- Date and time of application
- Reason for lockout/tagout
- Expected duration of work
- Warning statements about unauthorized removal

**Step 4: Energy Verification**
1. Test equipment controls in normal operating position
2. Equipment should NOT operate
3. Use appropriate test equipment to verify zero energy
4. Return operating controls to neutral position
5. Document verification results

**Verification Methods by Energy Type:**
- **Electrical:** Digital multimeter, voltage tester, test lamps
- **Pneumatic:** Pressure gauges, bleed valves
- **Hydraulic:** Pressure gauges, drain valves
- **Mechanical:** Visual inspection, attempt to move components
- **Thermal:** Temperature measurement, pressure relief

### 3.3 RESTORATION PROCEDURES

**After Work Completion:**
1. **Area Inspection:** Remove all tools, materials, and temporary installations
2. **Guard Replacement:** Ensure all machine guards are properly reinstalled
3. **Personnel Check:** Verify all workers are clear of dangerous areas
4. **Equipment Inspection:** Check that equipment is ready for normal operation
5. **Notification:** Inform affected employees of impending re-energization

**Lock and Tag Removal:**
1. Only the person who applied lock may remove it
2. If applier is unavailable, follow specific procedures for lock removal
3. Remove tags only after locks are removed
4. Remove lockout devices in reverse order of application
5. Operate energy isolating devices to restore energy

**Equipment Restart:**
1. Energize equipment in proper sequence
2. Test all safety systems before full operation
3. Verify normal operation of all controls
4. Monitor initial operation for any abnormalities
5. Document successful restoration

## SECTION 4: GROUP LOCKOUT PROCEDURES

### 4.1 MULTIPLE PERSON LOCKOUT
**When Multiple Workers Involved:**
- Each worker applies individual lock to lockout device
- Use multiple lockout hasp if necessary
- Primary authorized person coordinates overall procedure
- Each person responsible for their own lock
- No worker begins work until their lock is applied

**Group Lockout Box Method:**
1. Place all energy isolation keys in group lockout box
2. Each worker applies individual lock to box
3. Primary person retains master key to energy controls
4. Work cannot begin until all workers have applied locks
5. Each worker removes own lock upon completion

### 4.2 SHIFT CHANGE PROCEDURES
**Continuous Operations:**
1. Incoming authorized person applies lock before outgoing person removes theirs
2. Verify energy isolation is maintained during transfer
3. Document transfer in logbook with signatures
4. Brief incoming person on work status and hazards
5. Ensure continuity of protective measures

### 4.3 MULTIPLE LOCATION LOCKOUT
**For Complex Equipment:**
- Use master lockout list identifying all isolation points
- Assign specific isolation points to different personnel
- Ensure communication between all involved parties
- Use radio or phone communication for coordination
- Document completion of each isolation point

## SECTION 5: SPECIFIC ENERGY SOURCE PROCEDURES

### 5.1 ELECTRICAL ENERGY CONTROL

**High Voltage Systems (>600V):**
1. Open main disconnect and verify open position
2. Rack out circuit breakers to test position
3. Apply mechanical locks to prevent closure
4. Test with appropriate high voltage test equipment
5. Ground all phases if required by company policy

**Low Voltage Systems (<600V):**
1. Open circuit breakers or disconnect switches
2. Remove fuses if specified by procedure
3. Apply lockout devices to prevent closure
4. Test with digital multimeter or voltage tester
5. Check for back-feed from other sources

**Control Circuits:**
- Isolate control power separately from main power
- Lock out both primary power and control power
- Consider UPS and battery backup systems
- Test all control functions to verify isolation
- Use stored energy blocking devices where applicable

### 5.2 PNEUMATIC ENERGY CONTROL

**Compressed Air Systems:**
1. Close supply valve at source
2. Bleed air pressure from system through bleed valves
3. Lock supply valve in closed position
4. Verify zero pressure with calibrated gauge
5. Block cylinders to prevent movement

**Vacuum Systems:**
1. Close vacuum supply isolation valve
2. Break vacuum by opening atmospheric vent
3. Lock isolation valve in closed position
4. Verify atmospheric pressure in system
5. Block any vacuum-operated devices

### 5.3 HYDRAULIC ENERGY CONTROL

**Hydraulic Systems:**
1. Shut down hydraulic pump
2. Close supply and return isolation valves
3. Relieve pressure through pressure relief valve
4. Lock all isolation valves in safe position
5. Block hydraulic cylinders to prevent drift

**Hydraulic Accumulators:**
1. Isolate accumulator from system
2. Slowly bleed pressure through appropriate valve
3. Verify zero pressure with calibrated gauge
4. Lock isolation valves in closed position
5. Use mechanical blocking where required

### 5.4 THERMAL ENERGY CONTROL

**Steam Systems:**
1. Close steam supply isolation valve
2. Open drain valves to remove condensate
3. Allow system to cool to safe temperature
4. Lock isolation valve in closed position
5. Verify safe temperature before work begins

**Hot Water/Oil Systems:**
1. Shut down heating source (boiler, heater)
2. Close supply and return isolation valves
3. Allow sufficient time for cooling
4. Verify safe temperature with calibrated thermometer
5. Consider cooling water circulation if needed

## SECTION 6: VERIFICATION AND TESTING

### 6.1 ENERGY VERIFICATION METHODS

**Electrical Testing:**
- Use properly rated test equipment
- Test meter on known live source first
- Test each conductor to ground and phase-to-phase
- Verify test equipment function after testing
- Document all voltage readings

**Pressure Testing:**
- Use calibrated pressure gauges
- Verify zero pressure or specified safe pressure
- Check both static and dynamic conditions
- Test pressure relief devices if applicable
- Document all pressure readings

**Temperature Testing:**
- Use calibrated temperature measuring devices
- Allow sufficient time for thermal equilibrium
- Consider internal vs. surface temperatures
- Verify safe temperature throughout system
- Document temperature readings

### 6.2 PERIODIC INSPECTIONS

**Annual LOTO Program Review:**
- Review all energy control procedures
- Update procedures for equipment modifications
- Inspect lockout devices for condition and function
- Review accident and incident reports
- Update training materials as needed

**Lockout Device Inspection:**
- Check locks for proper operation and condition
- Verify tags are legible and securely attached
- Replace damaged or worn devices immediately
- Maintain adequate inventory of devices
- Test specialized lockout devices annually

## SECTION 7: TRAINING REQUIREMENTS

### 7.1 AUTHORIZED EMPLOYEE TRAINING

**Initial Training (8 hours minimum):**
- Purpose and function of LOTO program
- Recognition of hazardous energy sources
- Methods and means of energy isolation
- Proper application of lockout devices
- Verification of energy isolation
- Hands-on practice with actual equipment

**Annual Refresher Training (4 hours minimum):**
- Review of LOTO procedures and updates
- Practice with new or modified equipment
- Discussion of incidents or near-misses
- Testing of knowledge and skills
- Documentation of training completion

### 7.2 AFFECTED EMPLOYEE TRAINING

**Required Training Topics:**
- Purpose and use of LOTO procedures
- Prohibition against attempting to restart locked-out equipment
- Recognition of lockout devices and tags
- Reporting of defective or missing devices
- Understanding of consequences of unauthorized removal

### 7.3 CONTRACTOR TRAINING

**Before Work Authorization:**
- Verify contractor LOTO training and competency
- Review facility-specific procedures and requirements
- Ensure contractor has proper lockout devices
- Establish communication protocols
- Document contractor qualifications

### 8.2 INCIDENT REPORTING

**Reportable Incidents:**
- Unauthorized removal of lockout devices
- Equipment startup with personnel at risk
- Failure of energy isolation devices
- Injuries related to unexpected energy release
- Near-miss events involving energy control

**Investigation Requirements:**
- Immediate investigation of all incidents
- Root cause analysis and corrective actions
- Review and update of procedures if needed
- Additional training if deficiencies identified
- Documentation of lessons learned

## SECTION 9: EMERGENCY PROCEDURES

### 9.1 EMERGENCY LOCK REMOVAL

**When Original Applier Unavailable:**
1. Verify applier is not at facility and cannot return
2. Make all reasonable efforts to contact applier
3. Get supervisor authorization for lock removal
4. Verify equipment is safe for re-energization
5. Document emergency removal with justification

**Emergency Removal Procedure:**
- Cut lock with appropriate tools
- Complete thorough area inspection
- Verify all personnel are accounted for and safe
- Follow normal restoration procedures
- Notify original applier when contact is made
- Document incident and circumstances

### 9.2 MEDICAL EMERGENCY RESPONSE

**If Injury Occurs During LOTO:**
1. Ensure area is safe for rescue personnel
2. Do NOT remove lockout devices unless absolutely necessary for rescue
3. Call emergency medical services immediately
4. Provide first aid within scope of training
5. Notify management and safety personnel
6. Preserve incident scene for investigation

This comprehensive LOTO manual ensures safe energy control practices for all industrial maintenance activities.
