# Motor Maintenance Guide - Industrial Equipment

## SECTION 1: OVERVIEW
This guide covers maintenance procedures for AC induction motors, DC motors, and servo motors commonly found in industrial applications. Regular maintenance extends motor life and prevents costly breakdowns.

## SECTION 2: DAILY INSPECTIONS
Perform these checks at the start of each shift:

### 2.1 VISUAL INSPECTION
- Check for unusual vibration or noise during operation
- Inspect motor housing for excessive heat (>65°C normal operating temp)
- Look for oil leaks around bearing housings
- Verify motor mounting bolts are tight
- Check cooling fan operation (if equipped)
- Inspect electrical connections for signs of arcing or overheating

### 2.2 OPERATIONAL CHECKS
- Monitor motor current draw using ammeter
- Check operating voltage at motor terminals
- Verify smooth startup and shutdown
- Listen for bearing noise or grinding sounds
- Check belt tension (if belt-driven) - 1/2 inch deflection per 12 inches of span

## SECTION 3: WEEKLY MAINTENANCE

### 3.1 TEMPERATURE MONITORING
- Record motor operating temperature at nameplate location
- Normal range: Ambient + 40-60°C depending on motor class
- Class B insulation: Max 130°C (80°C rise + 40°C ambient + 10°C hot spot)
- Class F insulation: Max 155°C (105°C rise + 40°C ambient + 10°C hot spot)

### 3.2 ELECTRICAL CHECKS
- Measure and record phase voltages (should be within 1% of each other)
- Check current balance between phases (should be within 5%)
- Inspect conduit connections and junction boxes
- Verify proper grounding continuity

### 3.3 MECHANICAL INSPECTION
- Check coupling alignment using dial indicators
- Inspect flexible coupling elements for wear
- Verify belt condition and alignment (if applicable)
- Check for soft foot condition using feeler gauges

## SECTION 4: MONTHLY MAINTENANCE

### 4.1 INSULATION RESISTANCE TESTING
**Equipment Required:** Megohmmeter (1000V DC for motors up to 1000V)

**Procedure:**
1. Disconnect all power and lock out motor
2. Disconnect motor leads from starter
3. Test each winding to ground and between windings
4. Minimum acceptable values:
   - New motors: >100 megohms
   - Motors in service: >1 megohm per 1000V + 1 megohm
   - Below 1 megohm: Investigate further

### 4.2 VIBRATION ANALYSIS
- Use vibration meter to check overall levels
- Normal levels: <0.3 inches/second for motors up to 1800 RPM
- Record measurements at motor feet and bearing housings
- Look for increases >25% from baseline readings

### 4.3 LUBRICATION
**Ball Bearings (Sealed):**
- Typically maintenance-free for 40,000-50,000 hours
- Replace when noise increases or temperature rises

**Sleeve Bearings:**
- Check oil level in sight glass
- Oil should be at center of sight glass
- Change oil every 8,760 hours (1 year) minimum
- Use only specified viscosity oil

**Grease Lubricated Bearings:**
- Add grease through fittings while motor is running
- Amount: Bearing housing volume × 0.5
- Frequency: Every 2,000-10,000 hours depending on conditions
- Use only specified NLGI Grade 2 or 3 grease

## SECTION 5: TROUBLESHOOTING GUIDE

### 5.1 MOTOR WON'T START
**Possible Causes:**
- No power to motor terminals
- Blown fuses or tripped breakers
- Overload relay tripped
- Control circuit malfunction
- Motor starter contacts burned
- Single phasing condition
- Mechanical binding

**Diagnostic Steps:**
1. Check voltage at motor terminals with multimeter
2. Verify all three phases present (for 3-phase motors)
3. Check overload relay setting and condition
4. Test motor starter operation
5. Check for mechanical binding by hand rotation
6. Measure winding resistance with ohmmeter

### 5.2 MOTOR OVERHEATING
**Possible Causes:**
- Overloading
- Voltage imbalance (>2%)
- Single phasing
- Blocked ventilation
- Bearing problems
- Loose connections

**Diagnostic Steps:**
1. Measure actual current vs. nameplate rating
2. Check voltage balance between phases
3. Clean air passages and check fan operation
4. Check bearing temperature and noise
5. Tighten all electrical connections
6. Verify proper ventilation clearances

### 5.3 EXCESSIVE VIBRATION
**Possible Causes:**
- Misalignment
- Unbalanced rotor
- Worn bearings
- Loose mounting
- Bent shaft
- Resonance with foundation

**Diagnostic Steps:**
1. Check motor mounting bolt tightness
2. Perform precision alignment check
3. Vibration analysis to identify frequency
4. Check bearing condition
5. Inspect coupling for wear or damage

## SECTION 6: SAFETY PROCEDURES

### 6.1 LOCKOUT/TAGOUT REQUIREMENTS
- Always de-energize motor before maintenance
- Lock out main disconnect and motor starter
- Test with approved voltage tester
- Apply personal danger tags
- Wait for motor to come to complete stop

### 6.2 PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT
- Safety glasses required at all times
- Insulated tools for electrical work
- Leather gloves when handling hot components
- Hearing protection in high noise areas
- Arc-rated clothing for energized electrical work

## SECTION 7: MOTOR SPECIFICATIONS

### Standard NEMA Frame Sizes:
- 143T, 145T: 1-3 HP motors
- 182T, 184T: 3-7.5 HP motors  
- 213T, 215T: 7.5-15 HP motors
- 254T, 256T: 15-30 HP motors
- 284T, 286T: 30-50 HP motors
- 324T, 326T: 50-75 HP motors

### Standard Speeds (60 Hz):
- 2-pole: 3600 RPM (3450 actual)
- 4-pole: 1800 RPM (1750 actual)
- 6-pole: 1200 RPM (1150 actual)
- 8-pole: 900 RPM (850 actual)
